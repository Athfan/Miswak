Why This Works

The Science Behind Miswak

"Use the Miswak, for it purifies the mouth and pleases the Lord."

— Prophet Muhammad ﷺ (Sahih Bukhari)

For 1400 years this was faith. Today it is also peer-reviewed science. Over 300 published studies have now examined miswak (Salvadora persica) and confirmed what the Sunnah said. This alignment of religion and science is the single most powerful thing your brand can own.

🦠

Antibacterial

Kills Streptococcus mutans — the primary bacteria causing tooth decay. More effective than fluoride toothpaste in some clinical studies.

🌿

Natural Fluoride

Contains fluoride naturally — the same mineral dentists add to toothpaste — strengthening enamel without chemical additives.

🔬

Silica Abrasives

Micro-particles of silica in the bristles act as a natural whitening agent — gently polishing without damaging enamel.

💧

Increases Saliva

Stimulates saliva production which naturally neutralises acid in the mouth — the body's own cavity protection system.

🌱

Anti-inflammatory

Contains salvadorine and trimethylamine — natural anti-inflammatory compounds that reduce gum disease and bleeding.

✅

WHO Approved

The World Health Organisation officially endorsed miswak as an effective oral hygiene tool in its 1986 global dental health report.

The business opportunity is not to sell miswak sticks. It is to extract these proven compounds and deliver them in formats people already use — toothbrushes, toothpaste, mouthwash — making the Sunnah effortlessly accessible.

Manufacturing Process

1
SOURCING

Source Miswak Extract

Salvadora persica trees grow naturally in Rajasthan, Gujarat, and across the Middle East. Source dried miswak roots from wholesale suppliers in Rajasthan or directly from Saudi suppliers. The extract (powder or liquid) is available from herbal extract manufacturers in Ahmedabad and Delhi.

2
FORMULATION

Develop Your Formula

Work with a cosmetic chemist or an OEM manufacturer to develop your formulation. For toothpaste: miswak extract + calcium carbonate + glycerin + mint oil + xylitol. For toothbrush: bristles need to be dip-coated in miswak extract solution. Most OEM labs will do this for a one-time formulation fee of ₹10,000–25,000.

3
COMPLIANCE

Get Regulatory Approvals

Toothpaste falls under cosmetics in India — register under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act with CDSCO. Toothbrush is a consumer product — BIS certification is recommended. Your OEM manufacturer will typically guide you through this. Halal certification from Jamiat Ulama-i-Hind or HFCI adds major trust for your target market.

4
PACKAGING

Design & Packaging

This is where you win or lose on shelf. Invest in clean, premium packaging. Include: the Hadith quote in Arabic + English, the key ingredients, a "Sunnah + Science" badge, and halal certification logo. Use eco-friendly materials — it adds another marketing angle. Packaging design: ₹15,000–30,000 one time.

5
PRODUCTION

First Production Run

Place your first order of 500–1000 units. Quality-check samples before approving full batch. Store in a dry, cool warehouse space — even a room at home works for the first batch. Keep 3 months of supply on hand as your buffer once you start selling.

Where to Find OEM Manufacturers

  • IndiaMART.com — search "toothpaste OEM manufacturer" or "toothbrush private label"

Raising Capital

How to Pitch Investors

Once you have 6+ months of sales data, you can approach investors. Here is how to structure your pitch in 10 minutes.

Slide 1 — The Hook (30 seconds)

Open with a number that surprises them, then contrast it with the opportunity.

"Colgate has a 52% market share in India. They make $3 billion here annually. And they have never once mentioned that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ recommended an ingredient 1400 years ago that 45 studies have now proven outperforms their formula. We have. And in 6 months, we have sold X units with zero paid advertising."

Slide 2 — The Problem

200 million Muslims in India use toothpaste every day. Not a single mainstream brand speaks to their identity, their values, or their Sunnah. They buy Colgate by default — not by choice.

"Our customer has no option that is both halal-certified, scientifically superior, and rooted in their faith. We built that option."

Slide 3 — The Product

Show the product. Let them hold it if in person. Point to the Hadith on the packaging, the halal certification, the ingredient list. Then show the clinical study summary.

"This is not a religious product with science as a backup. This is a scientifically superior oral care product with a 1400-year head start in consumer trust."

Slide 4 — The Market

$52B global oral care market. 1.9 billion Muslims. Indonesia alone has 230 million Muslims. UAE, UK, USA diaspora all underserved. India is the pilot; global is the vision.

Slide 5 — Traction

Show your sales numbers, repeat purchase rate, customer testimonials, social media growth. Even small numbers tell a story if the trend is up.

"In 6 months, with ₹70,000 starting capital and zero paid ads, we have sold X units, achieved Y% repeat purchase rate, and built a community of Z followers. We are asking for ₹[X] to scale manufacturing, launch in UAE, and add two new product lines."

Slide 6 — The Ask

Be specific. Vague asks kill investor confidence.

"We are raising ₹25 lakhs at a ₹1.5 crore valuation. The capital will be deployed across three areas: 60% manufacturing scale-up, 25% digital marketing, 15% export market entry. This gives us 18 months of runway and takes us to ₹50 lakhs monthly revenue."
